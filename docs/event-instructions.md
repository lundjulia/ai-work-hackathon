​Claude Impact Lab — turning questions into working ideas
​Join a Claude Community Event, organized by local enthusiasts for everyone who loves building with Claude!

​A hands-on afternoon and evening exploring one central question: How is AI changing the way we work—and what should we build in response?

​This Impact Lab builds on the conversations and questions raised at Claude Conversations, back in August. Instead of stopping at discussion, we will turn the most important themes into practical experiments, prototypes, and concrete proposals.

​The format combines short inspiration, guided challenges, collaborative building, and a final demo session. Participants will work in small groups around a set of focused questions, using Claude and other AI tools to explore possible solutions. The goal is not polished products; it is to learn quickly, test ideas, and leave with something tangible.

​What to expect

​Short opening talks and an introduction to the Impact Lab challenges

​A structured, hands-on build session in small groups

​Guidance and support throughout the afternoon

​Informal food, snacks, and time to meet other participants

​Claude API credits to build and original merchandise from Anthropic for participants

​A closing demo hour when teams share what they explored or built

​From ideas to impact

​The Impact Lab is designed to be a starting point—not the end of the work. Every team will define the change it wants to create and leave with something that can be taken forward: a prototype or concrete concept, the people it is intended to help, and a clear next step.

​Alongside their solution, teams will create a simple 30-day impact plan with a named owner, the first real-world test, and the support needed to continue. During the demo hour, teams will share both what they built and what they will do next.

​After the event, the projects and commitments will be documented and shared with participants. Teams that want to continue can stay connected, invite new contributors, and report back on what happened. This gives the strongest ideas a path from an evening of experimentation to practical change.

​Who it is for

​The event is for developers, designers, product people, founders, operators, leaders, and curious AI practitioners who want to move from conversation to experimentation. You do not need to arrive with a finished idea or a team. Come ready to contribute, collaborate, and learn.

​The theme

​What does AI mean for my job?

​At the Claude Conversation in August, we asked what AI means for your job. On Wednesday we build the answers. In other words, how is AI changing the way we work, and what should we build in response?

​The four paths

​You pick a path when we form teams in the room.

​The person. Stay good at your work as AI takes over the tasks.

​The organization. Turn personal AI gains into shared practice, and decide which work stays human.

​The community and the city. Build tools that help people outside tech, and the city they live in, adapt as work changes.

​The open path. Bring your own problem about AI and work, and build it with a team.

​Before Wednesday

​Think about which path appeals to you. Write down one or two problems you’d like to solve, and who you’d solve them for. A user story works well. “As a [user], I want to [goal], so that [outcome].”
